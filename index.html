Watchmaking glossary

Short definitions for the terms used in the part guide and energy-flow tour.

Going train
The connected wheels and pinions that carry power from the mainspring barrel to the escapement.
Mainspring
A coiled metal ribbon that stores energy when wound and slowly releases it to drive the watch.
Torque
Turning force. The gear train trades higher torque for higher speed as energy moves toward the escapement.
Escapement
The escape wheel and pallet fork. Together they release the train one small step at a time instead of letting it run down freely.
Beat
One half-swing of the balance wheel. A 2.5 Hz movement beats five times per second.
Impulse
A brief push of energy from the escapement that keeps the balance wheel swinging.
Rotor
The weighted half-disc that swings when you move your wrist and winds an automatic watch.
Reversing wheels
Two-way coupling wheels that turn rotor motion in either direction into one winding direction.
Hairspring
The fine spiral spring that returns the balance wheel toward its centre and helps set the watch’s rate.
Jewel
A tiny synthetic ruby bearing that gives a wheel axle a hard, low-friction surface to turn in.
